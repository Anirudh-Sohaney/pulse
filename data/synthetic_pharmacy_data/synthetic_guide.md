# Synthetic Arkansas government-clinic pharmacy sales

## Deliverables and scope

`arkansas_clinic_daily_pharmacy_sales.csv` contains 32,880 rows: 30 medicines x every calendar day from **2023-01-01 through 2025-12-31** (1,096 dates, including leap day 2024). It represents dispensed units from a small local government clinic serving a low/medium-population Arkansas catchment. It is entirely synthetic; no patient, prescriber, or real clinic transaction is represented.

Each row contains date, setting, drug, therapeutic class, units sold, synthetic public-sector acquisition/dispensing price, revenue, stockout flag, and the exact injected event tags. `revenue_usd = units_sold * unit_price_usd`.

The data-generating process is deterministic (`random.Random(20250915)`) and can be regenerated with `python generate_synthetic_pharmacy.py`.

## What is and is not observed

Public sources do not publish daily sales for a named Arkansas government clinic. Therefore the baseline is a transparent synthetic calibration, not a claim about observed Arkansas sales. The structure follows the AHRQ MEPS prescribed-medicine event model: a prescription record has medicine identity, utilization/fills, days supplied, and expenditure/payment concepts. MEPS is nationally representative and can support utilization/expenditure estimates, but not this clinic’s daily counts. See [AHRQ MEPS 2023 prescribed medicines documentation](https://meps.ahrq.gov/data_stats/download_data/pufs/h248a/h248adoc.shtml) and [AHRQ MEPS 2022 file](https://meps.ahrq.gov/data_stats/download_data/pufs/h239a/h239adoc.shtml).

Baseline daily means are intentionally small (0.22–10 units/day per product) and mix chronic medicines with episodic products. This is a modeling choice for a small public clinic, not a published city-level sales estimate. It is anchored by: (1) MEPS’s national fill/expenditure framework, (2) CDC evidence that outpatient antibiotic prescribing is common and strongly seasonal, and (3) the selected clinic-like formulary mix (primary care chronic disease, respiratory/acute care, OTC relief, naloxone, and vaccines/antivirals). The random component is Poisson count noise, which is appropriate for low-rate daily event counts.

## Recent-news review (2023–2025)

The major unexpected events considered were: the 2022–23 tripledemic carryover; the end of COVID continuous Medicaid enrollment and Arkansas unwinding; the March 2023 OTC naloxone switch; the 2023–24 influenza peak in HHS Region 6; 2023–24 RSV/COVID co-circulation; the 2024 H5N1 dairy-cattle outbreak and first U.S. cow-to-human cases; the December 2024 severe Louisiana H5N1 case; Hurricane Helene’s September 2024 Baxter North Cove shutdown affecting IV fluids; and the U.S. summer-2025 COVID wave. The CSV applies only effects that are plausible for a small outpatient dispensary and marks scenario-only events explicitly. It does not invent Arkansas H5N1 cases, IV-fluid sales, or a generalized pandemic response.

## Validation notes

- Expected row count: `30 * 1096 = 32,880`; the generator prints the count.
- Expected date range: 2023-01-01 to 2025-12-31 inclusive.
- Expected products: 30, all repeated for every date.
- Counts are nonnegative integers; prices and revenue are nonnegative; revenue is recomputed from units and price rounded to cents.
- Every deterministic shock is exposed in `injected_event_tags`; weekends and event days are not silently removed.
- This is suitable for testing forecasting, anomaly detection, inventory logic, and dashboarding. It must not be used for clinical, reimbursement, procurement, or public-health decisions as if it were observed data.
